# Where the terms wording comes from

## What's on the site now

The sign-up form requires "I agree to the Terms and Privacy Notice", with a link to `/terms`. That page currently says, word for word: "These terms are being prepared from a published template and will appear here before launch."

**No terms text is on the site yet.** I didn't write my own legal text, and I didn't let an AI agent write it. The agent was told explicitly not to write any (see the Step 2 prompt).

## Where the wording will come from

1. **A published template from a free generator** (TermsFeed or Termly), for a small online clothing seller, with a privacy notice. I'll record the source URL and date here when I generate it.
2. **Adapted to what Tee actually does,** using the Task One interview. Tee collects names, emails and phone numbers. Prices are agreed per order. Changes and cancellations are decided case by case, with no fixed refund rule. Delivery is quoted per destination. Customers must be 18 or over to make an account.
3. **The privacy notice has to match the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023:** what data is collected, why, how long it's kept, and how a customer can ask for it to be deleted. (Tee said no customer has ever asked, but the right still exists.)
4. **Checked by someone qualified before launch.** That's ideally a Nigerian lawyer, or at least a small-business adviser. Until then, the page stays a placeholder, and the app isn't deployed.

## Why not just have the agent write it?

An AI agent will happily produce confident legal-sounding text. But it can't know Tee's real refund practice, it can invent obligations Tee can't meet, and nobody is accountable for it. Terms are a promise to customers. A template written by people whose job is legal text, adapted to Tee's real practice and then checked, is the honest route. "The agent wrote it and that's fine" isn't.
