WHAT GOT THROUGH, AND WHY IT MATTERS
- "a b" and "test test": two "words" beat the two-word rule. The rule checks shape, not
  whether it is a real name. A bot only has to add a space.
- "a@b.c": the right shape, but not a real address. Only sending a code proves ownership.
- "00000000000" and "+1 2345678": the right number of digits, but not real numbers.
- "       x" (7 spaces + x) and "aaaaaaaa": 8 characters, so the length rule is met,
  but they are trivially guessable. The rule stops ALL-space passwords, not mostly-space ones.
- Pasted spaces around name, email and phone: accepted, and that is intended. The form
  trims them (cleanFullName, cleanEmail, cleanPhone) before sending.

TWO WRONG MESSAGES FOUND (logged in docs/defects.md)
- An 81-character name says "Please enter at least your first name and surname",
  which is not the problem; it is too long.
- A 260-character email says "Check for a missing @ or dot", which is not the problem either.

All of this only matters for someone using the form. 16-direct-request-command-and-response.txt
shows that skipping the form skips every one of these rules.
